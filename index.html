    Focus

    Why short focused sprints can help

    The Pomodoro Technique itself hasn't been heavily studied as a branded method, but it borrows from well-supported ideas: working in short, focused bursts with real breaks can help manage mental fatigue and make it easier to resist checking your phone "just for a second." The break isn't wasted time — it's part of what makes the next sprint easier.

    SAT Countdown
    — — —

    days until your test date

    What to do with the time you have

    • 4+ weeks out: take a full practice test to find your baseline and weakest section.
    • 2–3 weeks out: drill your weakest section 20–30 minutes a day, not everything at once.
    • Final week: shift to review and sleep — cramming new material now adds stress without adding score.
    • Night before: no studying after dinner. Pack your bag, set two alarms, go to bed on time.

    AP Exam Countdown
    — — —

    days until your next AP exam

    Multiple AP exams? Don't split evenly.

    Spending equal time on every exam feels fair but wastes hours on subjects you've already got. Research on "desirable difficulty" suggests spending more time where you're weakest tends to help more than splitting time evenly — try pairing that with retrieval practice (flashcards, past FRQs) instead of re-reading notes.

    • Rank your exams by confidence, weakest first.
    • Do one full past FRQ per subject per week under timed conditions.
    • Interleave subjects across the week instead of one subject per day.

    For parents & caregivers

    Help your teen without another lecture

    What SCOPE does for your teen

    A private space with study tools and short, evidence-based guides on stress, sleep, focus, and motivation — used entirely on their own, with no account required.

    What SCOPE doesn't do

    It doesn't replace a therapist, doctor, or school counselor, and it never presents itself as a crisis line. If you're worried about your teen's safety, use the resources on our Crisis Resources page immediately.

    Three things that actually help, backed by research

    • Protect sleep first. Teens need 8–10 hours, and insufficient sleep is associated with problems involving attention, behavior, and learning.
    • Ask about process, not just grades. "What did you try?" builds resilience more than "What did you get?"
    • Normalize breaks. Downtime isn't laziness — it's part of how the brain consolidates learning.

    Qué hace SCOPE por tu hijo/a

    Un espacio privado con herramientas de estudio y guías breves basadas en evidencia sobre el estrés, el sueño, la concentración y la motivación — sin necesidad de crear una cuenta.

    Qué no hace SCOPE

    No reemplaza a un terapeuta, médico o consejero escolar, y nunca se presenta como una línea de crisis. Si te preocupa la seguridad de tu hijo/a, usa de inmediato los recursos en nuestra página de Recursos de Crisis.

    Tres cosas que realmente ayudan, según la investigación

    • Prioriza el sueño. Los adolescentes necesitan de 8 a 10 horas de sueño, y dormir menos de lo necesario está asociado con problemas de atención, comportamiento y aprendizaje.
    • Pregunta sobre el proceso, no solo las calificaciones. "¿Qué intentaste?" fortalece la resiliencia más que "¿Qué nota sacaste?"
    • Normaliza los descansos. El tiempo libre no es pereza — es parte de cómo el cerebro consolida el aprendizaje.

    About

    Real psychology and learning science, without the jargon

    SCOPE exists because most student mental-health and study-skills content is either a wall of text nobody reads or an app that needs a login before it's useful. We wanted neither.

    What does SCOPE mean?

    SCOPE stands for Student Center of Psychology and Education. It brings together practical academic tools (Education) with accessible psychology and stress-management resources (Psychology) — all in one place built specifically for students.

    SCOPE is an independent, student-focused educational project — not an official center, government program, university institution, or licensed healthcare provider. "Center" describes what it aims to be for students: one place to start, not a formal organization.

    Who it's for

    Middle and high school students first — with sections built for the parents, teachers, and counselors around them.

    What it does

    Combines short, evidence-informed resources, interactive tools like the Pomodoro timer and Class Stress Navigator, and a curated toolkit of trusted external resources.

    How we write

    Claims on SCOPE are written to reflect what research actually supports — including when evidence is limited, mixed, or from a single study. Sources are cited on resource pages.

    What SCOPE is not

    SCOPE is an educational resource for everyday academic stress and well-being — things like test anxiety, study habits, sleep, focus, procrastination, and burnout. It is not therapy, not a diagnostic or medical service, not a crisis counseling service, and not a substitute for a school counselor, therapist, doctor, or other professional. If you need one of those, SCOPE's job is to help point you toward it, not replace it.

    If you're in immediate danger or need crisis support, SCOPE isn't the right resource

    SCOPE is an educational tool for everyday academic stress and well-being — it does not provide crisis counseling and can’t respond to an emergency. Please use one of these established services instead. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, call 911 (US) or go to the nearest emergency room right now.

    988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

    Call, text, or chat 988 (US) — free, confidential support 24/7 for suicidal thoughts or emotional crisis.

    Crisis Text Line

    Text HOME to 741741 (US) to reach a trained crisis counselor by text, 24/7.

    Trevor Project (LGBTQ+ youth)

    Call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678678 — 24/7 support for LGBTQ+ young people.

    Emergency services

    If there is immediate danger to life, call 911 (US) or go to your nearest emergency room.

    Talk to a trusted adult

    A school counselor, teacher, coach, or parent can help you get further support quickly — you don't have to explain everything at once.

    Outside the US

    Visit findahelpline.com for a crisis line in your country.

    Privacy & Disclaimer

    Privacy & Disclaimer

    Plain-language version, not a long legal document. If anything here is unclear, that's a bug — let us know.

    Privacy

    • No account or login is required to use any part of SCOPE.
    • Some tools (favorites, the weekly planner, your Pomodoro count, your last-viewed resource) save data using your browser's local storage — on your device only. That data is never sent to a server and disappears if you clear your browser data.
    • SCOPE does not collect names, email addresses, grades, school records, or any mental-health information.

    Educational Disclaimer

    • SCOPE provides general educational information about study strategies and stress management.
    • SCOPE does not provide medical or psychological diagnosis, treatment, or therapy.
    • SCOPE does not replace a school counselor, therapist, doctor, or other licensed professional.
    • The Stress and Burnout Check-Ins are reflection tools, not clinical assessments.

    External Links

    • SCOPE links to external resources, including the Student Toolkit and citations on resource pages.
    • SCOPE does not control the content, availability, or policies of external websites.
    • Linking to a resource does not mean SCOPE is partnered with, sponsored by, or endorsed by that organization.

    Crisis

    • SCOPE is not an emergency or crisis service and cannot respond to an emergency.
    • If you're in immediate danger, use the resources on the Crisis Resources page or call 911 (US).
